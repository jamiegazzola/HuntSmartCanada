BC LEH Odds: Why One Year Can Mislead You

Draw odds are useful, but a single percentage can hide the story. Use HuntSmart's historical series to see how an opportunity changed before deciding what the latest number really means.

HuntSmart CanadaPublished Oct 26, 2026Updated Oct 26, 2026Interactive data · 7 min

If you are comparing BC Limited Entry Hunting opportunities, the latest draw percentage is usually the first number you notice. It should not be the last.

Applicant demand can change. Authorization numbers can change. Hunt structures can change. A regulation update or a sudden wave of attention can push hunters toward or away from a particular opportunity.

Historical odds do not predict the next draw. They give context. The value is seeing whether the latest year looks normal, unusually favourable, unusually difficult, or part of a longer trend.

What the chart is telling you

The useful question is not “which year was best?” It is “how stable has this opportunity been?” A historical series can reveal three very different patterns:

  • Stable: the odds stay within a fairly narrow range over many years.
  • Trending: the odds are gradually becoming easier or harder.
  • Volatile: the draw swings sharply, which makes one-year comparisons especially weak.

Why odds can change

Several moving pieces can alter a draw:

  • More or fewer hunters choose the hunt code.
  • The number of authorizations changes.
  • Season dates or hunt boundaries change.
  • Access, fires, weather, or hunter attention changes demand.
  • Nearby opportunities become more or less attractive.

Do not confuse draw probability with hunt quality

A hunt that is easier to draw is not automatically a better application. Odds answer one part of the decision. You still need to consider whether the area, season, access, travel, animal class, and hunt itself fit your goals.

How to use historical odds in HuntSmart

  1. Filter to the species and areas you can realistically hunt.
  2. Identify several opportunities that fit your schedule and access.
  3. Open the historical trend for each finalist.
  4. Compare the latest number with the longer history.
  5. Shortlist the hunts that make sense as complete opportunities — not just attractive percentages.
